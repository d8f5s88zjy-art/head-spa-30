# 020 — Publikum a pozícia produktu

Use when several possible customers or product stories compete for attention. Keep research claims separate from assumptions about an audience.

## Decide

Choose the primary visitor job and the situation that brings that visitor to the site. Distinguish a new prospect, returning customer, editor and account administrator. They may share a brand but need different information and interaction speed. Do not invent demographic precision unsupported by actual research.

## Execute

Use provided analytics, support questions, interviews or business knowledge to identify priorities and objections. Label inferred hypotheses. Map each segment to entry point, question, required evidence, action and failure consequence. Select a primary journey while giving secondary audiences a clear route.

Translate positioning into design choices: a specialist product may need precise comparisons; an appointment business may need location, availability and trust; a developer tool may need documentation and an immediate example. Match the cinematic layer to comprehension. An impressive intro that returning users must repeatedly wait through may be a poor fit.

## Verify

Walk the proposed page for at least the primary and one materially different visitor. Check whether the same CTA wording means different things to them. Use direct user feedback where available; do not present your own preferences as validated audience research. Evaluate clarity before speculative persuasion tactics.

## Deliver

Provide a short positioning statement, evidence-backed priorities, assumptions and the content consequences. Recommend one direction with its tradeoff. Define what new information would change the decision. Avoid manufacturing personas that add detail without changing what the team builds.
