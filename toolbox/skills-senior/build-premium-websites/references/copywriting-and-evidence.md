Use when the offer is vague, the interface depends on invented filler or important claims lack support. Treat copy as part of the functional design.

## Decide

Identify the visitor's question at each stage: what is offered, whom it suits, what it includes, what it costs and how to proceed. Match claims to an authorized source. Distinguish an aspiration, a fact, an estimate and a testimonial. Do not convert uncertainty into a confident marketing number.

## Execute

Write a clear primary proposition, concise supporting proof and an action label that predicts the real destination. Use actual services and language the audience understands. Separate pricing qualifiers and exclusions from decorative fine print. Keep essential conditions close to the decision they affect.

Request missing facts only when they prevent an accurate result; use clearly marked draft placeholders in review material when necessary, never unmarked production fabrications. Keep testimonials verbatim within permissions and retain attribution. Generated prose may improve clarity but cannot create reviews, credentials, partnerships or measurable outcomes.

## Verify

Read the page as a first-time visitor and follow each action. Check that text remains true when media fails, translated copy expands or the provider changes availability. Compare labels with the actual form or external booking step. “Book now” should not secretly be an unprocessed email request.

## Deliver

Provide final copy with a fact/source map for consequential claims, unresolved factual dependencies and editable placement. Use a content hierarchy rather than a wall of slogans. Record who maintains changeable details such as hours, services and prices so a polished launch does not immediately become misleading.
